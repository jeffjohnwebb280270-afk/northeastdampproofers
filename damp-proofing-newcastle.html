Why Newcastle homes get damp

Most damp we're asked about in Newcastle comes down to the age and type of the building. The city has a lot of housing that was built before cavity walls and modern damp-proof membranes, and each type has its own weak spots:

  • Tyneside flats. These two-storey terraces, split into an upstairs and a downstairs flat with separate front doors, are everywhere in Heaton, Sandyford, Fenham, Arthur's Hill and Benwell. Damp in the lower flat is often blamed on rising damp, but it's just as often a leak from the bathroom or kitchen above, a shared downpipe in the back yard, or yard paving laid up against the wall.
  • Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas. Jesmond, Heaton, Gosforth and parts of the West End have solid brick or stone walls that rely on drying out through their surface. Cement render, hard cement pointing, plastic masonry paint and raised garden beds can all trap moisture and push damp higher up the wall.
  • Cellars and basements. Many larger older houses in Jesmond and Gosforth have cellars. Damp, musty cellars are normal for their age, and whether you need tanking depends on what you want to use the space for.
  • Student houses and HMOs. Jesmond, Heaton and Sandyford have a lot of shared houses. Several people cooking, showering and drying clothes indoors puts a lot of moisture into the air, so condensation and black mould are the most common complaint we see in rented property.
  • Interwar and post-war homes in Kenton, Walker and Denton Burn usually have cavity walls, but cold lintels and poor ventilation still cause mould in bedrooms and bathrooms.

Long, cold, wet winters make any small defect in gutters, pointing or flashings show up as a damp patch. Our guide to a damp patch after heavy rain covers the usual culprits.

Common questions

Is the damp in my downstairs Tyneside flat coming from upstairs?

It can be. In a Tyneside flat, a leak from the upstairs bathroom or kitchen, or a shared downpipe in the back yard, can show up as damp on the ground floor and get mistaken for rising damp. We check the pattern of moisture readings, plumbing and drainage before blaming the ground. If the cause is in the upper flat, a written report helps both owners agree on the fix.

How much damp is normal in an old Newcastle cellar?

Some dampness is normal in an unconverted cellar below ground level. Whether you need to do anything depends on what you want to use it for. For storage, good ventilation and dealing with any obvious water entry may be enough. To turn it into a habitable room you would usually need a waterproofing system designed for that purpose. We'll explain the options before quoting for anything.

I rent in Jesmond or Heaton and my bedroom has black mould. Who should I contact?

Tell your landlord or letting agent in writing first, since they're responsible for dealing with damp and mould hazards (social landlords now have strict timescales under Awaab's Law). Black mould in shared houses is usually condensation, but it can be made worse by leaks or cold walls. Landlords and agents can book us for an independent survey and report. Our guide Black mould in winter: condensation or rising damp? explains the difference.

Do you survey stone villas and older houses in Jesmond and Gosforth?

Yes. Solid stone and brick walls need to breathe, so we look for things that trap moisture, like cement render, hard pointing, impermeable paints and raised ground levels, before recommending any treatment. If your home is listed or in a conservation area, check with Newcastle City Council before any work that could need consent.
