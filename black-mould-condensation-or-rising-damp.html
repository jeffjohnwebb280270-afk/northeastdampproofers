Black mould in winter: condensation or rising damp?

By Jeff Webb, PCA Qualified Technician in Damp & Timber · 6 November 2026

Short answer: if you're seeing black mould spots in corners, around windows or behind furniture, and it gets worse when the weather turns cold, it's almost always condensation, not rising damp. Rising damp looks different: a tide mark low down on ground-floor walls, white salts, and plaster that crumbles or bubbles. Getting this right matters, because the fixes are completely different and treating the wrong one wastes money.

Why it shows up now

Every winter we get the same calls across Newcastle, Sunderland, Durham and Gateshead. Heating goes on, windows stay shut, washing gets dried indoors, and the moisture we all produce from cooking, showers and breathing has nowhere to go. When that warm, wet air meets a cold surface it turns back into water. That's condensation, and mould spores love it.

The North East's older terraces and stone-built properties often have cold, solid walls and cold corners, so they're especially prone to it.

Signs it's condensation

  • Black mould spots, often in a speckled pattern
  • In corners, on window reveals, behind wardrobes and sofas, and on the ceiling in bathrooms and bedrooms
  • Water running down the inside of windows in the morning
  • Worse in winter, better in summer
  • Can appear upstairs as easily as downstairs

Signs it's rising damp

  • A tide mark on ground-floor walls, usually no higher than about a metre
  • White, fluffy salts on the wall surface
  • Plaster that's crumbling, bubbling or sounds hollow
  • Skirting boards that are rotting or stained
  • Present all year round, not just in winter
  • Never upstairs: it comes from the ground

Penetrating damp is the third possibility: water getting in from outside through a leaking gutter, cracked render, failed pointing or a roof defect. It usually shows as a patch that spreads after heavy rain, and can be at any height.

What to do about condensation

  • Use extractor fans when cooking and showering, and leave them running for a while after
  • Keep trickle vents on windows open
  • Dry washing outside or in a ventilated room with the door shut
  • Keep heating low and steady rather than blasting it on and off
  • Leave a gap between furniture and outside walls
  • Clean mould with a proper mould treatment rather than just wiping it, so it doesn't come straight back

If it keeps coming back despite all that, the property may need better ventilation, such as a positive input ventilation unit or continuous-running extract fans, or insulation to warm up cold spots.

What to do about rising damp

Genuine rising damp needs a proper diagnosis before any treatment. Plenty of walls that look like they have rising damp turn out to have a bridged damp-proof course, raised ground levels outside, or a leaking pipe. Those can be fixed far more cheaply than injecting a new damp-proof course.

Be wary of anyone who recommends injection work after a quick look with a moisture meter alone. Meters on their own can give misleading readings, especially on walls containing salts.

When to get a survey

If you can't tell which type you have, you're a landlord dealing with a tenant's complaint, or you're buying a house and the valuation mentions damp, a survey with moisture readings and a written report will tell you exactly what's going on and what it needs.

Common questions

Is black mould a sign of rising damp?

Usually not. Black mould almost always grows where condensation forms, on cold surfaces in rooms with moist air. Rising damp tends to show as tide marks, salts and crumbling plaster low down on ground-floor walls rather than black mould spots.

Will a damp-proof course stop condensation?

No. A damp-proof course is designed to stop moisture rising from the ground through the walls. Condensation comes from moisture in the air inside the home, so it needs better ventilation, steadier heating or extra insulation instead.

Do I need a damp survey?

If you can't tell which type of damp you have, or someone is recommending expensive injection work, a survey with moisture readings is worth having first. It means you pay to fix the actual cause.
