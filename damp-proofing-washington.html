Why Washington homes get damp

Washington was designated a new town in 1964 and built around older pit villages, so the housing is a real mix:

  • New town estates from the 1960s to the 1980s. Most of Washington's numbered "villages", such as Albany, Glebe, Oxclose, Lambton, Sulgrave, Biddick and Donwell, are cavity-walled houses and low-rise flats. True rising damp is rare in these. Black mould in bedrooms, on window reveals and behind wardrobes is usually condensation, made worse by weak extractor fans and cold spots at lintels. See our guide to condensation and black mould.
  • Retrofitted cavity wall insulation. Where added cavity fill has bridged the cavity or sits behind walls that take driving rain, damp can track through to the inside. See damp after cavity wall insulation.
  • Paths, patios and driveways built up against the wall. Paving, decking or soil raised to or above the damp-proof course bridges it and causes damp low on downstairs walls that looks like rising damp. The fix is often outside, not an injection. Read Has my damp-proof course failed?.
  • Older terraces in the original pit villages. Usworth, Concord, Columbia, Fatfield, Harraton and Springwell still have older brick terraces from the mining era. These often have solid walls, older damp-proof courses and sometimes suspended timber floors that need air bricks kept clear. See why blocking air bricks can rot your floor and damp patches on chimney breasts.
  • Stone cottages in Washington Village. The old village around Washington Old Hall has older stone buildings with thick walls built to breathe. Cement render, hard cement pointing and plastic masonry paint can trap moisture in them. See why cement and plastic paint trap damp in older walls.
  • Newer estates. Homes from Washington's later building phases and more recent developments, such as around Rickleton and Teal Farm, can still get damp, usually from condensation, poor ventilation or a defect such as a leak or bridged cavity tray. See damp in a new-build house.

Damp patches that appear after storms on exposed gables and around windows are more likely rain penetration than anything rising from the ground: see damp patches after heavy rain.

Common questions

I have black mould in my Washington estate house. Is it rising damp?

Probably not. Most of Washington's new town houses have cavity walls, and black mould in corners, window reveals and behind furniture is usually condensation. Better extraction and steady background heating normally help. If damp sits low on a downstairs wall, we check for bridging or a leak too. See Black mould in winter: condensation or rising damp?

My mortgage valuer says my Washington house needs a damp-proof course. Does it?

Not necessarily. High readings can come from condensation, salts, a raised path or a leak. A damp survey with proper testing tells you whether there's genuine rising damp.

I rent in Washington and have damp and mould. What should I do?

Report it to your landlord in writing. Social landlords have strict timescales under Awaab's Law, and private tenants have rights too. Meanwhile, see cleaning black mould safely. Landlords and agents can book us for an independent report.
